# 👥 HR Analytics Dashboard | Power BI

## 📌 Project Overview

This project presents an interactive **HR Analytics Dashboard** developed in Power BI to analyze workforce trends between **2013 and 2021**.

The dashboard provides insights into **employee headcount, retention, turnover, workforce demographics, departments, job levels, and termination patterns**. The objective is to transform employee and employment history data into clear workforce insights that can support HR planning and employee retention strategies.

## 🗂️ Data Structure Overview

The analysis is built using employee demographic data, employment history records, and a dedicated date table.

The main datasets contain information on:

- Employee demographics, including gender, age, race, education, and marital status
- Department, job level, and employee location
- Salary and employment status
- Hire and termination dates
- Termination type and reason
- Historical employment records

A dedicated **Date table** supports time-based calculations and allows HR metrics to be analyzed across different reporting periods.

## 📊 Executive Summary

### Overview of Findings

As of **2021, the organization had 3,414 employees**. Software was the largest department with **660 employees**, followed by Sales with **512 employees**.

The workforce was primarily made up of **Individual Contributors (2,555 employees)**, while **2,727 employees worked on-site** compared with **687 remote employees**.

Employee retention generally improved across the reporting period, reaching **97.6% in 2020** before declining slightly to **96.7% in 2021**.

Across the selected 2013–2021 period, the dashboard recorded **714 departing employees**, with an overall turnover measure of **37.4%**.

---

## 🔍 Insights Deep Dive

### 👥 Workforce Composition

Individual Contributors represent the largest employee group with **2,555 employees**, while Software is the largest department with **660 employees**. The workforce is predominantly on-site, and the largest age group is employees aged **30–39**.

### 📈 Employee Retention

Annual retention increased from **92.9% in 2013** to **97.6% in 2020**, before falling slightly to **96.7% in 2021**. Among the displayed job levels, **Team Leads recorded the highest retention rate at 91.8%**.

### 📉 Employee Turnover

Annual turnover peaked at **6.8% in 2014**, declined to **2.6% in 2019**, and increased again to **5.1% in 2021**. Directors and Managers recorded the highest displayed job-level turnover rates at **45.8% and 45.6%**, respectively.

### 🚪 Reasons for Leaving

The most frequently recorded reasons for employee departures were:

- **Found a better opportunity – 175 employees**
- **More flexible benefits – 173 employees**
- **Better salary – 146 employees**

These patterns highlight career opportunities, employee benefits, and compensation as important areas for further HR investigation.

---

## 💡 Recommendations

- Review **compensation, benefits, and career development opportunities** to address the most frequently recorded reasons for employee departures.
- Investigate turnover among **Manager and Director-level employees** to identify potential role-specific retention issues.
- Monitor the increase in annual turnover following the low recorded in 2019.
- Develop **department-specific retention strategies** for areas experiencing comparatively higher employee turnover.
- Regularly monitor headcount, retention, and turnover KPIs to identify emerging workforce trends.
